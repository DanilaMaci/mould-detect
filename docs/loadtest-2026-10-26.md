# Load test of services/api, 2026-10-26

First ramp tests of the own stdlib API (`services/api`) with Locust (`loadtest/`). Machine: an 8-core Mac, Python
3.14, Locust 2.47; load generator and API on the same machine. Raw results: `data/loadtests/<run>/` (local).
Scenario: 9/10 simulated sensor nodes (1 POST/s each), 1/10 dashboard viewers (~0.5 GET/s); +50 users every 10 s;
a step is broken at > 1 % failures or p95 > 1 s.

## Runs

| Run | Setup | Verdict | What it showed |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline64 | 1 Locust process, limit 64, nodes in sync | broke at 150 users | 503 shedding at ~150 req/s with p99 29 ms |
| inflight1024 | limit 1024 | broke at 1600 users | up to ~1450 req/s without failures; break = client error 49 |
| A_spread_64 / B_sync_64 | nodes spread vs in sync, limit 64 | 150 / 250 users | spreading the nodes did not help: the "synchronised bursts" explanation was wrong |
| C_spread_1024 | limit 1024, connection count added | broke at 1600 users | new connections explode above ~1000 users (1021 → 31 700) |
| (diagnostic) | 1200 users, 1 vs 2 Locust processes | — | 1 process: open connections stop at ~1024, the client closes and reopens them (4539 client closes, 0 server closes), 92 failures, 721 req/s; 2 processes: 1200 connections, 0 failures, 979 req/s |
| D_4proc_64 | 4 Locust processes, limit 64, peak-in-flight metric | broke at 150 users | peak in flight per second 87 at ~130 req/s (average ~2) |
| E_4proc_1024 | 4 processes, limit 1024 | broke at 2250 users | the API's real limit (below) |
| F_4proc_400 | 4 processes, limit 400 (new default) | broke at 2150 users (2.5 % shed) | no shedding below ~2000 req/s; at the cliff fast 503s instead of a meltdown |

## Findings

1. **Capacity of the own API on this Mac** (E): no failures up to ~2000 req/s offered. p50 12 ms and p95 under
   40 ms up to ~1500 req/s; p50 ~50 ms near 2000 req/s; at ~2100 req/s the cliff: p50 530 ms, p95 ~1 s, then p95
   5.9 s and 1.3 % "database busy" 503s (SQLite writers waiting > 5 s for the single write lock).
2. **The first break points were the load generator's.** One Locust process (gevent) held at most ~1024 open
   connections here; beyond that it closed and reopened connections, and the closed ones used up the Mac's local
   ports ("Can't assign requested address"). Measured: connection counts and close reasons (diagnostic run). The
   exact mechanism inside gevent is inferred, not verified. Fix: `ramp.py` uses 4 Locust processes by default.
3. **Load shedding must not close the connection** (own design flaw, fixed): a 503 that closed the connection made
   the client reconnect at once — a new connection and a new thread exactly when the server was overloaded. Now
   the small body is drained and the connection kept (tested).
4. **Size limits from peaks, not averages.** Little's law gives ~2 requests in flight at 130 req/s × 15 ms, but the
   measured per-second peak was 87 (D) and ~100–400 across E. The first default limit, 64, refused work the server
   could do from ~130 req/s. New default 400: above the peaks while still fast (~350 at 2000 req/s), below the
   meltdown peak (654). Cause of the clumping not established (candidates: thread scheduling under the GIL,
   connection bursts when users are spawned) [open].
5. **Shedding does its job at the cliff** (F vs E, same load ~2000–2100 req/s): with limit 400 the answered
   requests kept p95 94–120 ms and the excess got fast 503s (541); with limit 1024 p95 went to 990 ms and then
   5.9 s with slow "database busy" failures. Below ~2000 req/s limit 400 shed nothing.
6. **Monitoring must not be shed** (own design flaw, fixed): at the cliff `/metrics` and `/traffic.json` were refused
   or timed out like any request, so the numbers were missing exactly when needed (E and F have no
   `api_metrics.txt`). They now bypass the in-flight limit (tested while the server is full).
7. **Metrics bug** (own, fixed): clients disconnecting mid-request were counted as 500; now 499 (nginx's code).
8. **Measurement artefacts to know:** with several Locust processes the per-step req/s alternates high/low (workers
   report every ~3 s, steps are 10 s); pairs of steps average to the offered rate. `locust cpu` then shows only the
   master process.
